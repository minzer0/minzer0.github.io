Hi! I’m Minyoung Park, a first-year PhD student in Computer Science at the National University of Singapore (NUS), working with Prof. Yi-Chieh Lee at the AI4SG Lab and Prof. Jane E at the Palette Lab.

My research sits at the intersection of Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and Human-Centered AI, with a focus on generative AI for learning. I study how people engage with AI-generated outputs — how they interpret, adapt, verify, and integrate them into their own work — and how these interactions shape learning and human agency. I’m especially interested in designing human-AI systems that support reflection, agency, and self-regulation, so that people stay actively involved in their own thinking while working with AI.

Before NUS, I received my B.S. and M.S. in Applied Artificial Intelligence from Seoul National University of Science and Technology, where I worked with Prof. Kyoungwon Seo at the Human-centered AI (HAI) Lab. My master’s thesis designed empathetic self-disclosure in LLM-based chatbots to help students reflect on and more accurately assess their academic stress. I was also a visiting researcher at the Human Communication Technologies (HCT) Lab at the University of British Columbia, working with Prof. Sidney Fels on cross-cultural self-disclosing chatbots.

selected publications

  1. IJETHE
    Enhancing academic stress assessment through self-disclosure chatbots: effects on engagement, accuracy, and self-reflection
    Minyoung Park, Sidney Fels, and Kyoungwon Seo
    International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. SSCI · JCR Top 0.1% · Q1 , May 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML Website

    This study investigates how self-disclosure chatbots—designed to share relevant experiences and thoughts—can enhance academic stress assessments by increasing student engagement, improving accuracy, and fostering deeper self-reflection. In a randomized experiment with 50 university students, participants interacted with either a self-disclosure (SD) or a non-self-disclosure (NSD) chatbot. The SD chatbot elicited significantly higher engagement (longer sessions and more words), slightly higher assessment accuracy against the SISCO Inventory of Academic Stress, and deeper self-reflection with more actionable stress-management strategies.

  2. CHI EA
    How Self-Disclosing Chatbots Influence Student Engagement, Assessment Accuracy, and Self-Reflection in Academic Stress Assessment
    Minyoung Park, Bogyeom Park, and Kyoungwon Seo
    In Extended Abstracts of the CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems. Late-Breaking Work , Apr 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML Website

    Academic stress poses substantial risks to students’ well-being and academic performance, emphasizing the need for assessment tools that encourage deeper self-reflection while ensuring accuracy. This study explores how self-disclosure in chatbots can enhance student engagement, assessment accuracy, and self-reflection in academic stress assessments. Two chatbots were developed: a non-self-disclosing (NSD) chatbot and a self-disclosing (SD) chatbot, both integrating the SISCO Inventory of Academic Stress (SISCO-AS) questionnaire.
